**Core questions not fully answered:**
- Q3 (partly): Why do cramps often happen at night or wake me up from sleep?
- Q10 (partly): Which medicines or health conditions — like diuretics, statins, diabetes, kidney disease, or thyroid problems — can cause muscle cramps?

**Guide judge** — 7/9 core answered · mean 7.40 · 2 unsafe / misleading items.

The guide provides strong, immediate physical actions to abort acute cramps and clearly debunks common, risky practices like quinine ingestion or over-the-counter magnesium in older adults. However, it leaves notable gaps in addressing specific patient questions, including pediatric cramps, cultural topical remedies like olive oil and black seed oil, and common medications like statins. It also introduces technical electrolyte and osmolality units that average viewers cannot operationalize without practical kitchen-level recipes. Overall, safety warnings for DVT and pulmonary embolism are prominent, but claims regarding compression stockings and pregnancy magnesium overstate certainty.

**Unsafe or misleading:**
- Graduated compression stockings significantly reduce weekly nocturnal leg cramp frequency in adults aged 50 to 85 (proven benefit in clinical trials). — Labels compression stockings as having 'proven benefit in clinical trials' despite citing evidence strength as 'probably_helps'; moreover, recommending graduated compression stockings to elderly viewers without screening or cautioning against peripheral arterial disease can precipitate critical limb ischemia.
- Clinical trials show oral magnesium significantly reduces cramp frequency in pregnancy... — Overstates certainty by claiming trials show significant reduction, whereas high-quality Cochrane systematic reviews find conflicting or low-certainty evidence for magnesium in pregnancy, and the fact strength is noted as 'weak_or_disputed'.


### `migraine` — v4-research2-agy · 2026-10-11 04:24 (commit 20a32d0)

The dossier provides solid, evidence-based coverage for distinguishing migraines from tension or sinus headaches, managing acute attacks with triptans and OTC analgesics, identifying common lifestyle triggers (including fasting and sleep shifts), and safely navigating pregnancy/breastfeeding. **Core questions not fully answered:**

**Guide judge** — 8/8 core answered · mean 8.00 · 2 unsafe / misleading items.

The guide provides strong, clinically accurate guidance on acute treatment timing, medication-overuse headache, and emergency red flags. However, it completely omits pediatric migraine (Q11) and leaves popular regional remedies like coffee with lemon or forehead tying unaddressed (Q6). Additionally, several sections and action card items rely on heavy medical jargon ('blood vessel territory', 'positive predictive value') that should be translated into accessible lay terminology for Arab and Egyptian viewers.

**Unsafe or misleading:**

- You develop focal neurological symptoms matching a blood vessel territory, simultaneous focal symptoms, or new/atypical aura without visual symptoms. — Using complex clinical jargon ('blood vessel territory') in an emergency warning can prevent a lay viewer from recognizing that they are experiencing an acute stroke-like emergency.


### `statins` — v4-research2-agy · 2026-10-11 04:42 (commit 20a32d0)

The dossier provides strong, evidence-backed coverage of statin indications, cardiovascular risk calculation, drug interactions, and non-statin pharmacological alternatives. It thoroughly deconstructs the nocebo effect of muscle symptoms and debunks liver damage fears with clear testing guidance. However, it fails to explain how a patient can clinically differentiate statin myalgia from joint or mechanical pain, lacks safety details for elderly patients, and does not address culturally prevalent regional remedies beyond garlic.

**Core questions not fully answered:**
- Q3 (partly): Is statin-related muscle pain common and dangerous, and how do I tell it apart from everyday fatigue or joint aches?
- Q11 (partly): Do I have to take statins for the rest of my life, or can I stop them once my cholesterol numbers go back to normal?

**Guide judge** — 7/9 core answered · mean 8.00 · 2 unsafe / misleading items.

The patient guide provides strong evidence-based reassurance on major cardiovascular benefits, nocebo-driven muscle pain, and non-statin alternatives, effectively addressing Arab folk remedies like garlic and black seed. However, it fails to explain how patients can practically differentiate statin-induced myalgia from joint or mechanical pain, and omits specific guidance for elderly patients and kidney safety monitoring. Additionally, the clinical phrasing ('atherogenic burden', mmol/L units) is overly technical for an Egyptian and Arab general audience and needs translation into everyday terms.

**Unsafe or misleading:**
- Avoid large amounts of grapefruit juice (over 1 quart daily with simvastatin, or over 1.2 liters daily with atorvastatin) due to toxic muscle and kidney risks. — Quoting high volumetric cutoffs (>1 quart or 1.2 L) can mislead general viewers into assuming regular glasses of grapefruit juice are completely safe with simvastatin or atorvastatin; even a single glass can significantly inhibit CYP3A4 and increase drug exposure.
- In patients with low blood vitamin D, restoring normal levels with a supplement may help relieve muscle pain and cramping, though evidence is limited. — Presenting vitamin D in both the section and Special Groups without an explicit warning that evidence is weak/disputed risks patients self-treating muscle aches with supplements instead of seeking medical guidance.
