### Detailed Moment Breakdowns

#### MOMENT 1 — "إنذار الفجر" (The 3 AM Panic)
**Timestamp**: 0:00–0:45 (Hook)
**Type**: Relatable / Reveal
**Core Insight**: A low heart rate (45 bpm) during sleep is often a sign of a healthy, athletic heart, not a medical emergency.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: Starts with a high-anxiety scenario everyone with a smartwatch fears: waking up to a "danger" notification in the middle of the night.
**Curiosity Gap**: Is my watch lying to me, or is my body weird? What is the "one warning" that actually matters?
**Extraction Complexity**: Clean Extract.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: Safe. It validates that low HR during sleep can be normal but keeps the viewer engaged to hear the exceptions.
**Platform Notes**: Perfect for a "POV" style caption: "لما ساعتك تصحيك الفجر مرعوب..."

#### MOMENT 2 — "فخ الـ 5%" (The 5% Statistical Trap)
**Timestamp**: 1:45–2:45 (Act 1)
**Type**: Myth-bust / Shocking Statistic
**Core Insight**: Lab "normal ranges" are statistical agreements; 5% of perfectly healthy people will always fall outside those ranges.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: "عارف إنك ممكن تروح لمعملين مختلفين بنفس عينة الدم ويطلع واحد بيقولك طبيعي والتاني يحطلك علامة خطر؟" (Did you know you can get two different results from the same blood?)
**Curiosity Gap**: If the "normal range" is just an average, how do I know if *my* result is actually dangerous?
**Extraction Complexity**: Needs Re-Edit.
**Re-Edit Notes**: Start with the "two different labs" line. Use a split-screen visual showing two lab reports with different "Normal" ranges.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: Essential to include the line: "This is a statistical line, not a final judgment."
**Platform Notes**: High text-overlay potential. Use "95% vs 5%" graphics.

#### MOMENT 3 — "عداد سرعة جسمك" (The Sugar Speedometer)
**Timestamp**: 7:15–8:15 (Act 3)
**Type**: Myth-bust / Practical Takeaway
**Core Insight**: A blood sugar "spike" after eating is a normal physiological response (like accelerating a car), not an automatic diagnosis of diabetes.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: Calling out "Health Influencers" who scare people about eating fruit.
**Curiosity Gap**: If spikes are normal, what *should* I be looking at to know if I have insulin resistance?
**Extraction Complexity**: Needs Re-Edit.
**Re-Edit Notes**: Cut straight to the "Speedometer" analogy. It’s the strongest visual hook in the script.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: Must ensure the distinction between a healthy person's spike and a diabetic's chronic high sugar is clear.
**Platform Notes**: Use a car speedometer animation overlaying the doctor’s explanation.

#### MOMENT 4 — "إمتى تجري على الطوارئ؟" (The ER Red Line)
**Timestamp**: 9:45–10:15 (Act 4)
**Type**: Practical / High Stakes
**Core Insight**: Smartwatch data only equals an emergency when paired with specific physical symptoms (chest pain, dizziness, shortness of breath).
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: "إيه هو التنبيه اللي يستاهل تقلق بجد؟" (Which alert actually deserves your worry?)
**Curiosity Gap**: What are the specific symptoms that turn a "glitch" into a "life-saving alert"?
**Extraction Complexity**: Needs Re-Edit.
**Re-Edit Notes**: Needs a very fast-paced list of symptoms (وجع صدر، نهجان، دوخة) appearing as text on screen.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: **CRITICAL.** This is the most responsible clip to share. It prevents over-anxiety while ensuring people don't ignore real heart issues (AFib).
**Platform Notes**: Use "🚨" emojis and urgent red text. This is high-shareability "public service" content.
