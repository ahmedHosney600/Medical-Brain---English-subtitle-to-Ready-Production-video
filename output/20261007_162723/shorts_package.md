### Detailed Moment Breakdowns

#### MOMENT 1 — لغز الـ 28 ثانية (The 28-Second Mystery)
**Timestamp**: 0:00–0:50
**Type**: "Wait, what?" Reveal
**Core Insight**: Medical research uses an arbitrary 30-second cutoff for AFib, meaning a 28-second episode might not "count" scientifically, even if the heart felt the same.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: Dr. Ahmed looking at his watch and saying: "لو قلبك رفرف 30 ثانية بالظبط دي نوبة.. بس لو 28 ثانية، الرقم ده مبيتحسبش!" (If your heart flutters for 30s, it's an episode... but 28s doesn't count!)
**Curiosity Gap**: Why do those 2 seconds matter? Does it mean the person with 28 seconds is safe?
**Extraction Complexity**: Clean Extract.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: Safe. It frames the 30s as a "research rule" rather than a "safety rule," which is explained in the full video.
**Platform Notes**: Perfect for a "Talking Head" style with a text overlay: "28 vs 30 seconds."

#### MOMENT 2 — درجة النجاح في الطب (The Passing Grade)
**Timestamp**: 2:15–2:55
**Type**: Practical Takeaway / Myth-bust
**Core Insight**: Medical "normal ranges" are like school passing grades (50% vs 49%)—the difference is a tiny margin, not a total shift in health status.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: "الطالب اللي جاب 49% هل يفرق فعلياً عن اللي جاب 50%؟ الفرق شعرة، بس السيستم محتاج خط أحمر." (Does a student with 49% really differ from one with 50%? It's a hair's breadth, but the system needs a red line.)
**Curiosity Gap**: If medical numbers are arbitrary "red lines," how should we actually read our lab results?
**Extraction Complexity**: Clean Extract.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: High. This helps reduce "lab result anxiety" (Waswasa) without telling people to ignore doctors.
**Platform Notes**: Use a split-screen with a "School Grade" on one side and a "Blood Test" on the other.

#### MOMENT 3 — اللقطة ولا الفيلم؟ (The Snapshot vs. The Movie)
**Timestamp**: 3:15–4:10
**Type**: "Wait, what?" Reveal
**Core Insight**: Traditional medicine is a "snapshot" (10-second ECG), while wearables are a "movie" (24/7 data), and you can't judge a movie by one frame.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: Dr. Ahmed holding an old ECG paper: "زمان الطب كان لقطة.. دلوقتي ساعتك بتصور فيلم سينمائي لقلبك." (Medicine used to be a snapshot.. now your watch films a movie of your heart.)
**Curiosity Gap**: What happens when the "movie" shows something weird that the "snapshot" missed? Is it always a disease?
**Extraction Complexity**: Needs Re-Edit.
**Re-Edit Notes**: Overlay visuals of a flickering film strip vs. a single polaroid photo to emphasize the metaphor.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: Safe. It explains why more data isn't always "better" data without professional context.
**Platform Notes**: High visual potential. Use fast cuts between the "old" paper and the "new" watch.

#### MOMENT 4 — كارثة الرياضيين (The Athlete Paradox)
**Timestamp**: 6:45–7:35
**Type**: Myth-bust / Shocking Stat
**Core Insight**: A heart rate that looks "dangerously slow" (Bradycardia) on a watch is actually a sign of a very strong, athletic heart.
**Scroll-Stopper Factor**: "في الكتب، الرقم ده مرض.. بس عند الرياضي ده قمة الصحة!" (In books, this number is a disease.. but for an athlete, it's peak health!)
**Curiosity Gap**: If my watch says my heart rate is low while I'm sleeping, am I an athlete or am I in trouble?
**Extraction Complexity**: Clean Extract.
**Medical Responsibility Check**: Safe, but needs the caption: "استشير طبيبك لو فيه أعراض" (Consult your doctor if you have symptoms) to ensure non-athletes don't ignore true bradycardia.
**Platform Notes**: Use a "Health Alert" graphic that flips to a "Green Checkmark" for an athlete.
